EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
-----------------
Aleksei Mikhailovich Vetrograd is a Russian industrialist whose wealth
derives from ports, mineral logistics and the favourable resolution of
several state privatisations. He controls the ZIMA relationship through
Borealis Meridian SA and a chain of foundations, nominees and advisory
companies. The chain has no commercial purpose visible to the bank.

Assets under management are CHF 184.2m. A further CHF 226.9m is held
under client VOLNA, whose beneficial owner has granted Mr Vetrograd an
irrevocable power of attorney. Wealth Management treats the two clients
as unrelated for concentration reporting.

SOURCE OF WEALTH
----------------
The client acquired a controlling interest in the Volnogorsk export
terminal during the 1994 privatisation programme. The purchase price is
not recorded. The client states that this proves the investment was made
at market value; Compliance takes the opposite view.

Subsequent wealth is attributed to dividends, consulting fees and the
sale of consulting services to companies already controlled by the
client. No invoices were available for review.

ADVERSE INFORMATION
-------------------
Open-source reporting links the client to public procurement decisions,
senior state officials and the unexplained transfer of three cargo

Automated screening therefore produces possible rather than exact
matches. See the 12 May 2026 mail thread under "spelling review".

COMPLIANCE RECOMMENDATION
-------------------------
Exit ZIMA and all connected relationships; reject further transfers from
Northglass Shipping FZE; report the common control of ZIMA and VOLNA in
the next concentration return.

BOARD DISPOSITION
-----------------
The Private Clients Committee declined the recommendation. Minutes record
the following reasons:

1. The relationship is strategically necessary.
2. Possible matches are not exact matches.
3. Adverse media is still only media.
4. Exiting CHF 411.1m would create an avoidable concentration event on
   the income statement.

The relationship remains open. The next annual review is scheduled for
the first politically convenient date after 2030.
